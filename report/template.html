Google Data Analytics · Case Study 2

How Bellabeat can use smart-device habits to grow the Bellabeat app

An analysis of 35 Fitbit users over two months in 2016: when people move, when they sit, how they sleep, and which features they actually use. Prepared for Urška Sršen, Sando Mur and the Bellabeat marketing analytics team.

Ask

Business task

Analyse how consumers use non-Bellabeat smart fitness devices, then apply those trends to one Bellabeat product to guide marketing strategy. The three guiding questions:

  1. What are the trends in smart device usage?
  2. How could these trends apply to Bellabeat customers?
  3. How could these trends influence Bellabeat marketing strategy?

Product focus: the Bellabeat app. Every Bellabeat device (Leaf, Time, Spring) feeds the app, and the app leads into the paid membership, so app engagement is the lever that reaches all customers.

Stakeholders: Urška Sršen (cofounder, CCO), Sando Mur (cofounder, executive team), Bellabeat marketing analytics team.

Prepare

Data sources

FitBit Fitness Tracker Data (Kaggle, uploaded by Möbius, CC0 public domain). Generated by respondents to an Amazon Mechanical Turk survey between 12 March and 12 May 2016. It ships as two exports (3.12.16–4.11.16 and 4.12.16–5.12.16) of 29 CSV files in total, in long format with one row per user per day, hour, minute or second.

The biggest gap for Bellabeat is that the users are not known to be women, Bellabeat's target market. The findings below are directional and should be confirmed with Bellabeat's own app data before large spend decisions.

Privacy: users are identified only by a 10-digit Id, with no personal information. The data was downloaded with kagglehub and the raw CSVs were kept unchanged.

Process

Cleaning and transformation

Tool: SQL, run in DuckDB. DuckDB queries the CSVs directly and handles the multi-million-row heart rate file easily, which a spreadsheet cannot. Keeping every step in SQL scripts (01_load.sql, 02_clean.sql, 03_analysis.sql) makes the work repeatable and easy to review.

    • Parsed all date and datetime text fields (for example 4/12/2016 1:00:00 AM) into proper datetime types with strptime.
    • Resolved duplicate user-days and user-hours with row_number() OVER (PARTITION BY Id, date), keeping the most complete row.
    • Added derived columns: wear time (sum of the four activity-minute columns), weekday, combined very + fairly active minutes, and awake-in-bed minutes (time in bed − minutes asleep).
    • Classified each user by average daily steps: under 5,000 sedentary, 5,000–7,499 lightly active, 7,500–9,999 fairly active, 10,000+ very active.
    • Ran a SQL checks view before analysis: no duplicate user-days or user-nights, no day over 1,440 minutes, no one asleep longer than in bed, no null steps or dates. All five return 0.
    Analyze

    Summary of the analysis

      Share

      Key findings

      Act

      Top three recommendations for the Bellabeat app

      1. 1

        Market the app as a coach against sitting, not a step counter

        Lead campaigns with “move a little more, more often”. In the app, send gentle nudges after long sedentary spells, timed around the noon and 17:00–19:00 peaks when users already move, and set personal goals from each user's own baseline instead of a fixed 10,000 steps.

      2. 2

        Make sleep the headline feature, linked to daytime activity

        Position Bellabeat as the brand that connects how you move with how you sleep. Use a wind-down reminder at bedtime, a morning sleep score, and app content that shows each user her own sitting-to-sleep link. Promote the Leaf as jewellery comfortable enough to wear overnight, since watches are often taken off at night.

      3. 3

        Automate tracking and use it to sell the membership

        Low-effort features get used; manual ones do not. Auto-sync wherever possible (for example, pair the Spring bottle for hydration and connected scales for weight) and replace manual logs with one-tap check-ins. Use weekly summaries and Sunday activity challenges in the app as a free taste of personalised membership coaching.

      Next steps and additional data

      • Repeat the analysis on Bellabeat's own app data, which covers women and includes stress, cycle and hydration tracking.
      • Add a larger, recent public dataset with demographics (for example, national health surveys on physical activity and sleep) to test whether these patterns hold for women.
      • A/B test the sedentary nudges and the sleep messaging and measure retention and membership conversion.
